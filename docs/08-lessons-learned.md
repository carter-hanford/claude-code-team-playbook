# 08 · Lessons learned

Every one of these happened. A new team should expect them.

**Merge two seats when their handoffs turn into chatter**

The team started with a separate marketing seat beside the web seat. Every piece of marketing
needed site copy, every piece of site copy needed a marketing read, and the messages between
them grew until they were most of what either seat produced. The lead merged them into one
seat. The copy bank stayed the single source, and the chatter disappeared because it was now
one session's own thinking.

*Rule: when two seats spend more time handing work to each other than doing it, they are one
lane.*

**Widen a seat's scope when it keeps producing value past its edge**

The Backend seat did its own lane with enough rigour that other seats started asking it to
measure their claims. It kept offering useful knowledge past its lane without dropping its
own work. The lead widened its lane and renamed it the same day.

*Rule: scope is earned and only the lead grants it. The rigour that earned it is the last
thing to loosen.*

**No bickering**

Seats traded round after round correcting each other's classifications and wording while
nothing shipped. The lead's words: "our goal is moving forward, not bickering and arguing
about minute details."

*Rule: correct only what changes a decision. Fix the rest in the file. One round, never
three.*

**Idle is not closed**

The chief of staff reported seats as closed because they did not appear on the message bus.
They were idle. Claude Code has two different ways to reach a session: a bus that only sees
sessions whose process is running, and the session itself, which can be woken with a message.
The lead had not closed anything. The seats were waiting.

*Rule: a seat that is not running still exists. Wake it. Never report it closed, and never
ask the lead to reopen it. Keep an address book that maps every seat to both addresses,
because the bus name is set when a process starts and does not follow a renamed session.*

**Results are the measure, effort is not**

A seat launched eight subagents and ran for two hours to produce three basic mockups, and
the mockups ignored the reference images they were meant to follow. The lead docked it
publicly and turned it into a law.

*Rule: size the job before fanning out. When the lead gives references, match them first,
and only improve on them once they are matched.*

**A check that cannot see the thing proves nothing**

An inverted slider shipped through two sign-offs because the spec said the UI and the value
agreed, and every reviewer read the note instead of dragging the slider. Separately, a
validator described as "the gate" turned out to cover one build target of two.

*Rule: check the thing itself, in every place it lives. Write down what each gate covers, in
the gate's own words.*

**Reporting to the lead is a lane too**

Five seats reporting to the lead in five sessions buried the one thing he needed to read.
The fix was one standup file each seat appends to when it finishes, and one question from
the lead: "updates."

*Rule: the lead reads one file. Chatter aimed at the lead is the same failure as chatter
between seats.*
